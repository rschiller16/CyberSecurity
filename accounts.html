Why two-factor authentication matters

Two-factor authentication (2FA) asks for two different kinds of proof when you sign in. Your password is something you know. A security key you plug in or tap is something you have.

What if someone gets your password?

Imagine that you enter your campus password on the fake mailbox page from the phishing example. Someone now tries that password on the real campus portal.

  • With a password alone: The stolen password may be enough to open your inbox and read password-reset emails for other accounts.
  • With a security key as your second factor: The portal also asks for your registered key. The person with your password cannot complete that sign-in with the password alone.

If you entered your password on a fake page, change it through the real campus portal and tell campus IT, even if you had 2FA turned on.

Turn on 2FA

  1. Open your account through its usual app or website.
  2. In security settings, look for “two-factor authentication” or “two-step verification.”
  3. Follow the steps to register a security key or an authenticator app.

Only approve sign-ins you started. Keep one-time codes to yourself.
